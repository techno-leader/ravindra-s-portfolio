The Problem

Paper registers lose patients.

Most private dental clinics in India still record every visit in a paper register. Clinics that have gone digital usually keep an Excel sheet. Neither is a secure medical record, and both end the same way: the pages fill up, the register goes on a shelf, and nobody opens it again.

So nobody calls the patient back. A patient who needed a follow-up, a second sitting or a six-month cleaning simply does not return, and for a dental clinic the returning patient is where most of the revenue is.

Research

Why clinics stay on paper.

I started with a dentist I worked with: their register, their staff and their day at the chair. Then I ran demo calls with clinics that had already paid for software. Digital tools exist. Dentists don't use them for four reasons.

Finding 01There is no time at the chair

A patient is waiting. Any tool that takes longer than writing in the register is dropped within days.

Finding 02The staff are not technical

Records are kept by front-desk staff, and most of them have never used practice software.

Finding 03Software is costly and generic

One dentist was paying ₹70,000 a year for Practo to run two clinics, on software built for general physicians.

Finding 04Years of records already exist

No clinic starts from zero. Old registers and sheets have to come along, or the new tool is never adopted.

How it was built

Three tries before the app.

It started with one dentist's paper register. Before writing an app, we tried the cheapest tools available. Each one failed for a reason that ended up shaping Dentomate.

The Product

Built for the chair, the desk and the patient's phone.

Each part of the app answers one of the findings. The screens below are the shipped product.
